## The viewport runs the engine

The viewport is not a preview drawn by the editor. It is a Dacha world. It runs the engine's
own `Renderer` and `CameraSystem` over your scene's actors. Sorting layers, opacity and blending
modes look exactly as they will in the game.

That world does not run your code. Your systems and behaviors do not run, and no collision is
resolved. You edit the scene here, and you watch the game itself in the
[browser tab](/introduction/how-it-works/).

The view you pan and zoom belongs to the editor. It is not the `Camera` the game looks
through.

## Debug layers

Some parts of a scene draw nothing: a collider, a checkpoint, an actor that only carries data.
The debug layers draw them in the editor only. Turn them on in *View → Settings*.

**Colliders** outlines every `Collider` in the scene. The color comes from the collider's
`debugColor`, and green is the default.

**Markers** draws an icon for each actor that has an `EditorMarker` component. Add it to a
spawn point or a checkpoint, and you can see and click that actor in the viewport. The game
ignores `EditorMarker`.

## The bottom bar

*Reload Required* appears when you add or remove a sorting layer. The renderer builds its
layers once at startup, so the editor has to reload to pick them up.

## Settings

*View → Settings* holds the grid, the debug layers and the theme. The grid step there is also
the step that *Snap to Grid* uses.
