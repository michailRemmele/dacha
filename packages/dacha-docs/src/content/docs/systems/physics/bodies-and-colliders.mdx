A `Collider` gives an actor a shape. A `RigidBody` makes the actor a body that the physics system
moves or that other bodies stop at. See [what each combination does](/systems/physics/#what-each-combination-does).

## Collider

A collider has one shape. The shape fields depend on `type`:

| `type` | Fields | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `box` | `size` | A rectangle, `size.x` wide and `size.y` tall |
| `circle` | `radius` | A circle |
| `capsule` | `height`, `radius` | A vertical capsule. `height` is the distance between the centres of the two round ends, so the full height is `height + 2 * radius` |
| `segment` | `point1`, `point2` | A line between two points |

Every collider also has these fields:

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `offset` | Moves the shape away from the actor's position |
| `layer` | The [collision layer](/systems/physics/collisions/#collision-layers). Layers decide which colliders collide |
| `debugColor` | The colour of the collider in the editor's *Colliders* [debug layer](/editor/interface-tour/#debug-layers) |
| `disabled` | Turns the collider off |

All sizes are in world units, in the actor's local space.

### Transform and colliders

The shape follows the actor's `Transform`. Position, rotation and scale all apply, and the offset
turns with the actor.

- A box scales by `scale.x` and `scale.y` separately.
- A circle and the round ends of a capsule stay round. Their radius scales by the larger of
  `scale.x` and `scale.y`.

### Segments

Use segments for ground and walls that do not move. A segment has two limits:

- Two segments never collide with each other.
- `castActor` finds nothing for an actor with a segment collider, because a line with no
  thickness cannot be swept.

### One collider per actor

An actor has at most one `Collider`, and a body uses only the collider on its own actor. Colliders
on child actors are not part of the parent's body.

For a shape made of several parts, such as a long wall, use several actors, each with its own
collider and body.

### Changing colliders at runtime

The physics system reads the collider in every step. Change `offset`, `layer`, `disabled` or a
field of `shape`, and the next step uses the new value:

```ts
const collider = actor.getComponent(Collider);

if (collider.shape.type === 'circle') {
  collider.shape.radius = 20;
}
```

To change the shape type, replace the component:

```ts
actor.setComponent(
  new Collider({
    type: 'box',
    size: { x: 32, y: 16 },
    offset: { x: 0, y: 0 },
    layer: 'default',
    disabled: false,
  }),
);
```

## Body types

`RigidBody.type` is one of three types:

| Type | How it moves | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `static` | By your code | Ground, walls, anything fixed |
| `dynamic` | By the simulation | Crates, balls, debris |
| `kinematic` | By your code | Moving platforms, doors, elevators |

A `static` body is part of the level. The simulation never moves it, and no contact can push it.

`type` is read-only. To change it, replace the component.

## Dynamic bodies

A `dynamic` body is a physical object. Gravity pulls it, forces push it, and other bodies push it
away. The simulation decides where it ends up, not your code.

These fields control how a `dynamic` body moves:

| Field | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mass` | `1` | How hard the body is to push. With `0` or less, the body does not move at all |
| `gravityScale` | `1` | Multiplies gravity for this body. `0` ignores gravity, `2` doubles it |
| `linearDamping` | `0` | Slows the movement over time |
| `angularDamping` | `0` | Slows the rotation over time |
| `lockRotation` | `false` | Stops the body from rotating |

Damping removes a part of the velocity in every step. At `linearDamping` `1` and the default 50
steps per second, the body loses 2% of its speed per step.

### Surface fields

These fields apply to every body type:

| Field | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `friction` | `0.6` | How much the surfaces resist sliding |
| `restitution` | `0` | How much the body bounces, from `0` to `1` |

When two bodies touch, the contact uses the higher `restitution` of the two, and friction from
both. A body with `friction` `0` slides on any surface.

### Velocity

`linearVelocity` is the velocity in world units per second. `angularVelocity` is the rotation
speed in radians per second. The editor does not show them.

### Forces and impulses

`RigidBody` has methods to push a `dynamic` body:

| Method | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `applyForce(force, position?)` | Pushes the body during the next step |
| `applyImpulse(impulse, position?)` | Changes the velocity at once, by `impulse / mass` |
| `applyTorque(torque)` | Rotates the body during the next step |
| `applyAngularImpulse(impulse)` | Changes the rotation speed at once |
| `clearForces()` | Removes the forces and impulses that are not applied yet |

`position` is a point in world space. A force or an impulse away from the body's centre also
rotates the body.

The physics system applies forces once, in the next step, and then clears them. For a constant
push, apply the force in every `fixedUpdate`:

```ts
// in your behavior or system
fixedUpdate(): void {
  this.actor.getComponent(RigidBody).applyForce(this.force);
}
```

Use an impulse for a single push, such as an explosion or a hit.

The methods do nothing when the body is not `dynamic`, or when it is disabled. `applyTorque` and
`applyAngularImpulse` also do nothing when `lockRotation` is on.

## Kinematic bodies

A `kinematic` body moves where your code tells it, and it pushes `dynamic` bodies out of the way.
Nothing pushes it back, so it never leaves its route. That is what a moving platform or a door
needs: a `static` body would not push the crate standing on it, and a `dynamic` one would fall
down.

Move a `kinematic` body in one of two ways.

`movePosition(target)` moves the body to `target` in the next step. The physics system calculates
the velocity for that step, so the body pushes `dynamic` bodies on its way. After the step, the
velocity is zero again. Call it in every `fixedUpdate` while the body moves:

```ts
const body = actor.getComponent(RigidBody);

body.movePosition(new Vector(x, y));
```

`linearVelocity` moves the body at a constant velocity. The body keeps moving until you change the
velocity.

Gravity, forces and impulses do not affect a `kinematic` body.

## Moving a body through Transform

You can change a body's `Transform` directly. The body is at the new position at once, like a
teleport. Its velocity does not change, and it does not push other bodies on the way.

Use it to place a body, for example at a spawn point. To move a body smoothly, use velocity,
forces or `movePosition`.

## Disabling a body

With `disabled` on, the physics system does not move the body. It clears the forces applied to
it, and other bodies pass through it.

## Bodies in a hierarchy

The physics system moves bodies in world space. A body can be a child only of an actor that does
not move. If the parent moves, both the parent and the physics system change the child's position,
and the result is undefined.

## One-way bodies

A **one-way** body stops bodies from one side, and lets them pass from the other side. Use it for
platforms that a character jumps onto from below.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `oneWay` | Turns one-way on |
| `oneWayNormal` | The direction to the side that is blocked |

For a platform you can land on from above, set `oneWayNormal` to
`{ x: 0, y: -1 }`:

- A body that falls onto the platform from above stops on it.
- A body that comes from below passes through.

The normal turns with the actor.

A body that starts to pass through keeps passing through until the two bodies separate. So a body
that jumps through the platform from below does not get stuck in the middle.
